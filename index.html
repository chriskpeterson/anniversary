A Note on Method

This Chart & a Standard Birth Chart

This reading is built on an inception chart — a birth chart cast for the moment a relationship began rather than for a person. It is a legitimate and longstanding astrological tradition, but it differs from a standard natal chart in ways worth naming.

What They Share

Both chart types use the same sky, the same planets, the same signs and houses, and the same interpretive grammar. A Sun placement in an inception chart is read the same way as a Sun placement in a personal chart — its sign, its degree, its aspects to other planets all carry meaning. Transits (the ongoing movement of planets through the sky) apply to both chart types identically. In that sense, the craft is the same.

The Subject Is Different

A natal chart maps the character, psychology, and life path of a person. An inception chart maps the character and trajectory of an entity — in this case, a marriage. The planets do not describe Chris's personality or Andrea's inner life; they describe the nature of the relationship itself: how it loves, how it fights, how it grows, what it is built to become. The Moon conjunct Jupiter here does not belong to either individual — it belongs to what exists between them.

No Rising Sign

A natal chart's Ascendant — the Rising sign — requires a precise birth time and location to calculate, and it shapes how the chart presents itself outward to the world. Inception charts can technically carry a Rising sign (this marriage began at a known time and place in College Station, TX), but this reading does not foreground it. The Sun at 0° Aries is so cosmically dominant — sitting at the World Point itself — that it effectively performs the function of both Sun and Ascendant, describing simultaneously the marriage's inner nature and its outward identity.

No Individual Psychology

A natal chart's Mars describes how you pursue what you want. The Mars in Sagittarius in this inception chart describes how the marriage moves forward — its shared drive, its appetite for adventure, its philosophical restlessness. This is a meaningful distinction. Two people with very different personal Mars placements can share a marriage whose Mars is entirely Sagittarian in character. The inception chart captures something neither individual contains alone.

A Composite Chart: The Other Approach

The most common alternative method for charting a relationship is the composite chart, in which each planet's position is calculated as the mathematical midpoint between the same planet in each partner's natal chart. A composite Sun, for instance, falls at the exact midpoint between Chris's natal Sun and Andrea's natal Sun. This technique maps the relationship as the territory between two people rather than as a discrete birth event. Both methods are valid; they tend to illuminate different things. The inception chart emphasizes the quality of the moment the relationship entered the world. The composite chart emphasizes the dynamic that exists between the two individuals. This reading uses the inception approach — and for Chris and Andrea, the moment of entry was extraordinary enough to justify making it the center of gravity.

Why the Inception Chart Works Here
A marriage that began at 0° Aries — the Vernal Equinox, the World Point, the first breath of the entire zodiac — carries an inception chart so distinctive that it warrants reading on its own terms. The moment of beginning was not arbitrary. Whatever drew Chris and Andrea to the equinox as their wedding date, consciously or not, placed their marriage at one of the most symbolically charged coordinates in the astrological calendar. That placement is the foundation of everything in this reading.
